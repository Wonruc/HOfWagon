# HOfWagon
HOf Gauge Wagon 3D Files and Instructions CC BY 4.0
HOf Gauge Wagon
~ 10 Scale Feet Long

This should be able to roll through 45 mm Rokuhan curves.  The wheel spacing and coupler distance were set to allow the car to go around these tight curves while coupled without interfearance.  

Requires Two Z scale wheels and two Rokuhan long shank couplers (ROK-A025 recommended)

The wheels and couplers can also be scavenged from a Rokuahn ROK-SA003 or ROK-SA004 or ROK-SA006 doner chassis.

3D Printed Parts
HOfWagonChassis: 1
HOfWagonSideFrame: 2
HOfWafonCouplerPocket: 2
HOfWagonBody: 1

Printing Recommendations

This car is printed in 5 parts not counting the optional loads.  The default orientation of these parts in the .stl files is their print ordination, and should require no supports or brim.  It is recommended to print the main flatcar chassis with 100% infill for added weight.

Recommended parameters
Filament: PLA 
Nozzle: 0.4 mm
Layer Height: 0.12 mm
Initial Layer Height: 0.2 mm
Strength>Wall Loops: 3
Recommend a smooth build plate, but a textured plate will work.

It is also recommended to print by object rather than by layer.  This setting can be found under the global Others>Print Sequence setting.

It is also recommended that if you are using the Bambu Slicer to switch to Orca Slicer, keep your printer in LAN mode, and install the Open Bamboo Network Plugin.  Bambu has already tried to lock users out of third party apps once, and there is no way they are not going to comply with proposed laws like California’s AB-2047 which would also require them to make their printers only work with their software. (https://github.com/ClusterM/open-bamboo-networking#downloads)



Assembly:

Step 1: Insert the Truck Side Detail Frames into their slots.  These should be friction fit, but if they are loose, it is ok to glue them. I insert the side with the brake handle first and then use the flat handle of tweezers to push in the other side.



Step 2: The coupler pockets  have a thicker base on one side that is inserted into it’s slot in the car.  These are not friction fit so that you have to glue them.  This is to provide strength when you are inserting or removing the couplers, and so the coupler pockets wont fall off the car in use.



Step 3: Insert the wheels and the couplers.  Insert the wheels on one side at an angle into the pocket for the wheel point, and then press the other side in.  Now you are done with a perfectly functional flat car if you want to stop here.



Step 4: Place the body onto the chassis.  The rivet details on the side ends of the flat car chassis provide friction with the body to hold it in place.  No glue is required.



Step 5: (Optional)  I have provided a few loads for the open wagon.  They should just drop in or press in.
